# Which is better for authorizing an AI agent, a policy engine or Proofable?

A policy engine is better when your own team owns both the rules and every caller, and a log inside your infrastructure is enough evidence. Proofable is better when the decision has to be checked before the action runs and confirmed afterwards by a system you do not control.

## It depends

Keep your policy engine if you already express your rules in it, one team owns both the rules and every caller, and a log inside your infrastructure is enough evidence. Add Proofable when the agent runs somewhere you do not own, when more than one party has to rely on the outcome, or when the evidence has to travel with the action. A policy engine decides; Proofable can supply the portable record that the decision was made under current permission. Teams that already have a policy engine usually keep it and add Proofable at the boundary, rather than replacing it.

## What each one answers

| Question | Policy engine or API gateway | Proofable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is this action allowed by our rules? | Yes, static role and tool rules (46 of 48 on AgentGovBench) | Yes, plus owner-to-agent delegation, spend cap, expiry and instant revoke (48 of 48) |
| Who is the agent and who owns it? | Only if you modelled it there; a gateway sees an API key in flight | Signed owner-to-agent identity and scoped permission |
| Does the decision survive leaving your system? | Server log row inside your infrastructure | A signed receipt (`qHash`) another agent or auditor verifies offline |
| Can a downstream agent trust a handed-off result? | No receipt layer; agents trust forwarded text or redo the work | Verifies the signed receipt in 3.7 ms ($0.024 vs $0.078 redo; 3 of 3 poisoned handoffs caught) |
| Does a refused action stop the call? | Depends on your integration | The caller stops before the tool runs (6 of 6 takeover exfiltration attempts blocked) |

## Where they fit together

A policy engine is the right owner of your own rules. Proofable does not ask you to move them. It checks the agent's limits at the action boundary and records the result, so the same decision can be appraised by a system that never saw your engine.

```ts
import { evaluateRuntimeAction } from '@proofable/sdk/runtime-mount';

const decision = evaluateRuntimeAction(mount, 'publish_report', { irreversible: true });
if (!decision.allowed) throw new Error(`${decision.code}: ${decision.message}`);
```

Your engine can still run before or after this check. [Standards and interoperability](/learn/standards) lists the authorization and attestation layers Proofable works beside, including AuthZEN and AARM.
